# UC-03: Evidence-grounded prospective research

## Record status

- **Identifier:** `UC-03`
- **Definition status:** Draft
- **Observed basis:** prospective research planning for `ksdft2effmass`
- **Evidence architecture:** [search](../architecture.search.md) and
  [applications](../architecture.applications.md)
- **Workflow architecture:**
  [planned workflow boundary](../architecture.md#planned-architecture)
- **Parent roadmaps:** `RAG-ROADMAP-01` and `WORKFLOW-TRANSFER-01`

This record describes a product use case. It does not authorize a computation,
source acquisition, contract acceptance, software change, manuscript edit,
scientific acceptance, publication, release, or deployment.

## Goal

A researcher uses Project Koios to develop a new research question, examine the
existing evidence, state a prospective hypothesis, design a bounded calculation
or study, and evaluate later observations against criteria recorded before the
outcome was known.

The system helps preserve the distinction between:

- what prior literature reports;
- what earlier local calculations observed;
- what the researcher proposes;
- what a protocol predicts;
- what a new run actually produces; and
- what interpretation is made after observing the result.

An LLM may help discover, compare, plan, or explain. It cannot turn a proposal
into a finding or a computed observation into verified literature support.

## Independence from other use cases

UC-03 is not downstream of UC-01 or UC-02. It can begin with any authorized
combination of reference evidence, prior computational artifacts, research
questions, and software baselines admitted by their owning contracts.

The three use cases may reuse ingestion, retrieval, provenance, workflow, and
review infrastructure. Shared infrastructure does not create a dependency or
execution order among them.

## Why this is a distinct use case

Textbook question answering seeks an evidence-grounded explanation. Manuscript
development seeks author-controlled prose and citation decisions. Prospective
research instead seeks a **future discriminating observation**.

Its defining sequence is:

```text
existing evidence
  -> unresolved question
  -> prospective hypothesis
  -> bounded protocol and decision criteria
  -> separately authorized execution
  -> immutable observations
  -> comparison with the prospective record
  -> reviewed interpretation
```

The protocol and evaluation criteria must exist before the result they are used
to evaluate. Later changes create new revisions rather than silently rewriting
the prospective record.

## Actors

### Primary actor

**Researcher** — defines the scientific question, reviews evidence, owns the
hypothesis, and makes the scientific interpretation.

### Supporting actors

- **Scientific reviewer** — challenges assumptions, protocol design,
  falsifiability, and interpretation.
- **Ingestion system** — produces immutable evidence from literature,
  calculations, data, and run outputs.
- **Search system** — retrieves source and computational evidence with corpus
  and status filters.
- **Generation client** — proposes questions, hypotheses, protocols, or
  interpretations from bounded evidence.
- **Workflow system** — records engine-neutral plans, transitions, and compact
  artifact references.
- **Computation worker** — performs an externally authorized bounded operation.
- **Deployment adapter** — resolves private storage and performs authorized
  effects outside the workflow core.
- **Local review interface** — presents evidence, plans, observations,
  discrepancies, and decisions.

## Preconditions

1. Machine-local configuration identifies authorized private data and artifact
   roots.
2. Relevant literature and prior computational artifacts have immutable
   identities and provenance.
3. Source evidence, prior observations, plans, generated proposals, and new
   observations have distinct corpus roles.
4. Any software baseline is identified by repository, revision, environment,
   and dependency evidence appropriate to the operation.
5. Existing calculation outputs remain distinguishable from manuscript text
   and from literature evidence.
6. Workflow planning is effect-free.
7. Every execution attempt requires fresh, attempt-bound external authority.
8. The prospective record can be made immutable before execution begins.
9. Private questions, inputs, outputs, paths, credentials, and payloads remain
   outside public logs and fixtures.

## Trigger

The researcher identifies a possible research direction, unresolved behavior,
model limitation, discrepancy, or hypothesis relevant to the research program.

Examples include:

- testing the range of validity of a reduced physical model;
- comparing an approximation against a higher-fidelity calculation;
- varying a parameter to locate a transition or failure boundary;
- distinguishing two explanations that fit existing observations;
- reproducing a prior computational result under controlled conditions;
- checking whether a source convention changes a derived quantity;
- investigating sensitivity to numerical resolution or modeling assumptions;
  or
- designing a calculation whose negative result would still be informative.

## Main success scenario

### 1. Establish the research context

1. The researcher records a bounded research question.
2. The question receives an immutable identity and an initial revision.
3. Search retrieves separately:

   - literature evidence;
   - prior local calculation evidence;
   - relevant methodological or software evidence; and
   - known warnings, disagreements, and insufficiency findings.

4. Manuscripts and generated notes may contribute query context, but they do
   not become literature evidence.
5. Curated distillations may assist discovery, but final factual support
   resolves to source evidence or an identified computational artifact.
6. Search returns bounded evidence bundles with exact locators, ranks, corpus
   roles, statuses, and warnings.
7. Missing literature, unavailable data, or absent computational provenance is
   recorded as a gap rather than silently inferred.

### 2. Form a prospective hypothesis

8. The researcher states a hypothesis or a set of competing hypotheses.
9. Each hypothesis identifies:

   - the proposed relationship or expected behavior;
   - scope and applicable regime;
   - assumptions and source conventions;
   - prior evidence motivating it;
   - a predicted observation;
   - observations that would challenge it;
   - known alternative explanations; and
   - unresolved dependencies or risks.

10. An LLM may propose hypotheses or counterarguments, but each remains
    `AUTOMATED_UNREVIEWED` until the researcher reviews it.
11. Hypothesis acceptance for testing does not establish scientific validity.

### 3. Design the study

12. The researcher creates a protocol revision that defines:

    - input artifact identities;
    - software and environment identities;
    - model and approximation choices;
    - parameter ranges and units;
    - controls or reference calculations;
    - numerical tolerances and convergence checks;
    - expected output artifact kinds;
    - resource and time bounds;
    - stopping conditions;
    - failure and ambiguity handling;
    - evaluation metrics; and
    - decision criteria for the predicted outcomes.

13. The protocol distinguishes exploratory parameters from confirmatory
    parameters.
14. Any adaptive step states in advance what evidence permits the adaptation
    and what bounds still apply.
15. A reviewer may challenge the protocol for confounding, underdetermination,
    circularity, missing controls, convention mismatch, or inadequate bounds.
16. The accepted-for-execution prospective package contains exact references
    to the question, evidence snapshot, hypothesis, protocol, and review
    disposition.
17. The package is sealed as an immutable revision before execution authority
    is requested.

### 4. Authorize and execute

18. The workflow planner deterministically produces an effect-free operation
    plan containing compact artifact references.
19. Runtime policy verifies operation identity, subject, scope, bounds, and the
    currently applicable external authority.
20. A fresh dispatch authorization is bound to one execution attempt.
21. The computation worker receives only the inputs and capabilities admitted
    for that attempt.
22. Runtime history records append-only dispatch, progress, completion,
    failure, cancellation, and reconciliation events.
23. Source material, prior evidence, and the sealed prospective package remain
    unchanged.
24. The worker emits output artifacts and receipts without declaring the
    hypothesis correct.

### 5. Ingest observations

25. The ingestion system identifies and verifies each output artifact.
26. It emits an immutable observation generation containing, as applicable:

    - raw numerical outputs;
    - logs and warnings;
    - input and environment references;
    - convergence evidence;
    - derived tables or plots;
    - failed or incomplete outputs; and
    - transformation and exclusion records.

27. Derived summaries, visualizations, and model explanations remain separate
    from raw observations.
28. Negative, null, failed, or surprising results remain visible and are not
    dropped because they do not support the hypothesis.

### 6. Evaluate prospectively

29. The evaluation processor compares observations with the decision criteria
    from the sealed protocol revision.
30. It records metric values, passed or failed checks, missing observations,
    deviations, and ambiguity without rewriting the original criteria.
31. Deviations from the protocol are explicit and produce a distinct analysis
    status or follow-up revision.
32. The researcher reviews the source evidence, protocol, observations, and
    evaluation record together.
33. The researcher records a bounded disposition, such as:

    - supported within the tested scope;
    - challenged within the tested scope;
    - inconclusive;
    - invalid run;
    - protocol revision required;
    - replication required; or
    - new hypothesis suggested.

34. The disposition is not automatically generalized beyond the tested
    parameters, model, software, and evidence scope.

### 7. Continue the research program

35. A follow-up question or study creates new hypothesis and protocol revisions.
36. New prospective records may cite prior observations as computational
    evidence while preserving their original run identities and limitations.
37. Manuscript development, software changes, release, and publication remain
    separately authorized activities.

## Evidence and lifecycle roles

| Role | Meaning | Authority limit |
|---|---|---|
| `reference` | Literature or other authoritative source evidence | Does not establish that the source is scientifically correct |
| `prior_observation` | Immutable output of an earlier study | Valid only for its recorded inputs, environment, and scope |
| `research_question` | Bounded unresolved question | Not a claim of novelty or importance |
| `hypothesis` | Prospective explanatory or predictive proposal | Not an observed result |
| `protocol` | Prospective method and decision criteria | Does not authorize execution |
| `execution_plan` | Deterministic effect-free operation plan | Not dispatch authority |
| `runtime_event` | Append-only execution history | Does not establish scientific validity |
| `raw_observation` | Direct run output with provenance | May still be incomplete or defective |
| `derived_analysis` | Calculation, table, plot, or metric from observations | Must retain derivation evidence |
| `interpretation_proposal` | Proposed scientific meaning | Unreviewed until dispositioned |
| `research_disposition` | Human scope-bounded conclusion | Not publication or universal acceptance |
| `manuscript_material` | Writing context or later communication | Never evidence for itself |

No status transition may silently convert one role into another.

## Prospective record requirements

Before execution, the sealed prospective package must identify:

- research-question identity and revision;
- evidence-bundle identities used to motivate the study;
- hypothesis identities and competing alternatives;
- scope, assumptions, and source conventions;
- input and software-baseline identities;
- protocol and parameter-space revision;
- controls and convergence expectations;
- resource and safety bounds;
- predicted outputs;
- evaluation metrics;
- decision criteria;
- planned exclusions;
- review disposition; and
- unresolved risks.

A timestamp alone is insufficient. The package needs immutable content identity
and an append-only event showing that the identified revision preceded the
execution attempt.

## Retrieval behavior

### Literature and computation remain separate

A literature source may motivate a model. A computation may test it. Retrieval
must not merge these into one evidence class or imply that one verifies the
other.

### Prior results are scope-bound

A prior result is retrieved with its code, environment, input, convergence, and
warning references. Similar filenames or plot shapes are not enough to claim
replication.

### Research gaps are findings about the corpus

Failure to retrieve evidence means only that the admitted corpus and retrieval
process did not supply it. It does not prove novelty, falsity, or absence from
the scientific literature.

### Equation and convention awareness

Retrieval preserves the exact source convention, symbolic representation,
units, coordinate system, and approximation regime needed to compare equations
or calculated quantities.

### Adversarial planning

The system should make assumptions and alternative explanations retrievable so
that a protocol can discriminate among them. Retrieval quality is not measured
only by support for the preferred hypothesis.

## Workflow and effect rules

- Planning is deterministic and effect-free.
- Workflow state contains compact typed references, not source bytes, private
  paths, full protocols, or result payloads.
- Structural validation checks operation and subject binding only.
- Authenticity, expiry, revocation, applicability, and capability checks belong
  to runtime or application policy.
- At most one active occurrence for a run revision may be effect-eligible.
- Every effect attempt requires fresh, attempt-bound dispatch authority.
- Expired authority, a lost worker, or a timeout does not prove that an effect
  was not applied.
- Ambiguous effects enter query-only reconciliation and are not retried merely
  because a lease expired.
- Runtime history is append-only authority. Disposable databases may project
  it but do not replace it.

## Alternative and failure scenarios

### A. Evidence is insufficient to motivate the hypothesis

The system records the gap. The researcher may perform broader retrieval,
request acquisition, narrow the question, or retain an explicitly speculative
hypothesis.

### B. Retrieval appears to establish novelty

The system rejects that inference. Corpus absence and retrieval failure do not
establish novelty.

### C. Competing sources use incompatible conventions

The prospective package records each convention and the transformation, if
known. The system does not compare numerical values until compatibility is
established.

### D. The protocol changes after sealing

The old revision remains immutable. The researcher creates and reviews a new
revision, and any later run identifies which revision governed it.

### E. Software or dependencies change

The run uses a new software-baseline identity. Results from different baselines
remain distinguishable until compatibility or replication is demonstrated.

### F. Execution completion is ambiguous

The system performs query-only reconciliation. It does not automatically retry
the computation.

### G. The run violates bounds or convergence criteria

The output remains preserved, but the evaluation records an invalid,
incomplete, or inconclusive run rather than treating it as hypothesis evidence.

### H. The result contradicts the preferred hypothesis

The negative result is retained and evaluated against the prospective criteria.
The system does not rewrite the hypothesis or suppress the observation.

### I. An unplanned pattern appears

The pattern may motivate a new exploratory hypothesis. It is labeled
post-observation and cannot be represented as a prospective prediction from the
completed run.

### J. A model proposes an unbounded or unauthorized study

The proposal remains inert. No operation is planned or dispatched until bounds,
review, and external authority are supplied.

## Candidate artifact flow

```text
research question
  + literature evidence
  + prior computational evidence
  -> prospective hypothesis revision
  -> reviewed protocol revision
  -> sealed prospective package

sealed package
  -> effect-free workflow plan
  -> externally authorized attempt
  -> append-only runtime history
  -> immutable raw observations
  -> derived analysis

prospective criteria
  + observations and analysis
  -> evaluation record
  -> human research disposition
  -> follow-up question, manuscript input, or software task
```

These names describe use-case artifacts. Exact schemas and operation identities
remain contracts owned by their component repositories.

## Required provenance

Every evaluation must make it possible to recover:

- question, hypothesis, and protocol revisions;
- evidence snapshot used before execution;
- software repository and revision identity;
- environment and dependency identity;
- input artifact identities and parameters;
- authority and execution-attempt references;
- append-only runtime events;
- raw output identities and receipts;
- transformations, exclusions, and warnings;
- evaluation code and metric identities;
- deviations from the prospective protocol;
- interpretation proposal identity; and
- human disposition and exact tested scope.

Private paths, credentials, source excerpts, unpublished inputs, and result
payloads remain in private storage and outside public coordination records.

## Acceptance evidence for the pilot

A private reviewed pilot for the research program must include:

- a question motivated by literature and prior calculations;
- at least one competing explanation or falsifying observation;
- a sealed hypothesis and protocol revision created before execution;
- explicit parameter, resource, and stopping bounds;
- identified code, environment, inputs, and controls;
- a successful bounded run;
- a failed, incomplete, or deliberately simulated failure path;
- immutable raw and derived artifacts;
- evaluation against pre-recorded criteria;
- a negative or inconclusive outcome path;
- a protocol-revision path; and
- an ambiguity-reconciliation fixture that does not retry automatically.

The pilot is technically demonstrated only when evidence shows:

1. the prospective package predates and identifies the execution attempt;
2. the source, prior artifacts, and sealed package remain unchanged;
3. planning performs no external effect;
4. dispatch authority is fresh and attempt-bound;
5. runtime history and artifact receipts replay deterministically;
6. outputs resolve to exact inputs, code, environment, and protocol revision;
7. raw observations remain separate from derived analysis and interpretation;
8. negative, null, failed, and surprising outcomes remain visible;
9. evaluation uses the pre-recorded metrics and decision criteria;
10. deviations and post-observation hypotheses are labeled explicitly;
11. literature support and computational evidence remain distinct;
12. ambiguous effects enter reconciliation instead of automatic retry;
13. no private payload enters public logs or fixtures; and
14. no scientific, manuscript, software, release, or publication decision is
    inferred from technical completion.

Scientific success is not an acceptance criterion. A well-provenanced negative
or inconclusive result can satisfy UC-03 better than an irreproducible positive
result.

## Postconditions

On success:

- the research question and motivating evidence are inspectable;
- the hypothesis and decision criteria are demonstrably prospective;
- the executed study is bounded and attributable to exact inputs and software;
- raw observations and derived analyses are immutable and replayable;
- evaluation is traceable to criteria chosen before the outcome;
- negative and inconclusive results remain first-class records;
- interpretation is explicitly scope-bounded; and
- follow-up research, writing, or software work begins from durable evidence
  rather than reconstructed memory.

## Out of scope

UC-03 does not authorize or require:

- autonomous selection of a research agenda;
- claims of novelty based on retrieval absence;
- autonomous scientific acceptance;
- unbounded parameter sweeps or compute spending;
- automatic source acquisition;
- automatic software modification;
- automatic manuscript editing or citation insertion;
- suppression of negative or failed results;
- retroactive rewriting of hypotheses or criteria;
- publication, release, deployment, or artifact publication;
- CPN authority; or
- treating workflow completion as scientific validity.
