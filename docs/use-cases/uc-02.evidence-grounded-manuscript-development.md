# UC-02: Evidence-grounded manuscript development

## Record status

- **Identifier:** `UC-02`
- **Definition status:** Draft
- **Observed basis:** equation-aware citation review for a developing scientific
  manuscript
- **Architecture:**
  [evidence-grounded scientific retrieval](../adr.20260918.evidence-grounded-scientific-rag.md)
- **Parent roadmap:** `RAG-ROADMAP-01`

This record describes a product use case. It does not authorize manuscript
edits, citation acceptance, contract acceptance, implementation, publication,
or release.

## Goal

An author develops or revises a scientific manuscript with assistance from
retrieval, source inspection, citation review, and evidence-grounded drafting.
The system helps the author find relevant literature, compare it with a claim,
and prepare reviewable prose or citation proposals.

The manuscript remains author-controlled. The system never treats manuscript
text as supporting evidence for itself and never inserts prose or citations
without separate authorization.

## Independence from other use cases

UC-02 is not downstream of UC-01 and does not require UC-01 to complete first.
It begins with a manuscript-centered writing need and any reference corpus
admitted by the evidence-grounded retrieval architecture.

```text
manuscript claim or writing need
  + authorized reference evidence
  -> reference-only retrieval
  -> citation or drafting proposal
  -> human review
  -> separately authorized manuscript action, if any
```

UC-01 and UC-02 may reuse the same ingestion, evidence-unit, retrieval, and
provenance contracts. That shared infrastructure does not create a dependency
or execution order between the use cases.

## Actors

### Primary actor

**Author** — defines the writing need, evaluates evidence, makes scientific and
editorial decisions, and controls the manuscript.

### Supporting actors

- **Citation reviewer** — evaluates whether proposed sources support a claim.
- **Ingestion system** — supplies immutable source-linked evidence.
- **Search system** — retrieves reference evidence and reports ranking details.
- **Generation client** — proposes prose, structure, questions, or citations
  from bounded evidence.
- **Local review interface** — presents manuscript context and source evidence
  without reading private storage directly.
- **Bibliographic system** — reports citation identities and whether a key is
  present in the manuscript bibliography.
- **Deployment adapter** — performs authorized local reads, writes, and
  reconciliation outside the workflow core.

## Preconditions

1. The manuscript is available through an authorized read-only input.
2. Its exact byte identity or revision identity is recorded.
3. Original TeX, Markdown, equations, labels, and citation commands remain
   available without lossy rewriting.
4. Reference sources have immutable identities and evidence-linked retrieval
   units produced under an admitted ingestion contract.
5. Corpus roles distinguish the manuscript from reference evidence.
6. Search can require `corpus_role = reference` for citation support.
7. Bibliographic identities and citation-key presence can be checked without
   changing the bibliography.
8. Decisions are stored as append-only revisions bound to the manuscript
   identity under review.
9. Private manuscript text, paths, source excerpts, and review decisions remain
   outside public logs and fixtures.

## Trigger

The author requests help with one or more of the following:

- outline or develop a section;
- explain the literature background for a passage;
- locate sources for an existing claim;
- assess whether a citation supports an equation or statement;
- distinguish a calculated result from a literature-backed claim;
- compare conventions or results across sources;
- identify unsupported or over-broad statements;
- identify required sources that are absent from the local corpus; or
- prepare a source-grounded revision for human consideration.

## Main success scenario

### Manuscript preparation

1. The deployment adapter reads the manuscript without modifying it.
2. The system records the manuscript content identity and revision under
   review.
3. The manuscript parser preserves exact source text, TeX, equations, labels,
   citation commands, and source spans.
4. The parser identifies bounded writing units such as sections, paragraphs,
   equations, claims, and existing citations.
5. Automated claim identification or classification is recorded as
   `AUTOMATED_UNREVIEWED` until reviewed.
6. Manuscript units are assigned the corpus role `query_material`; they are not
   admitted to the reference-evidence index.

### Information-need construction

7. The author selects a passage, claim, equation, or planned topic.
8. The system constructs a retrieval request while retaining a link to the
   exact manuscript span and revision identity.
9. The request distinguishes, where possible:

   - literature claims;
   - definitions or conventions;
   - manuscript-derived calculations;
   - numerical observations;
   - methodological descriptions; and
   - editorial transitions or exposition.

10. An LLM may propose a query or classification, but its proposal cannot
    establish claim type, support, or acceptance.

### Reference-only retrieval

11. Search executes against reference evidence only. The manuscript, generated
    prose, prior model answers, and citation proposals are excluded as evidence.
12. Curated notes may assist discovery, but final support resolves to an
    authoritative source page or span.
13. Retrieval may use lexical, equation, or accepted semantic lanes according
    to their documented policies.
14. Candidate results are diversified by source so that repeated chunks from
    one document do not crowd out alternative evidence.
15. Search returns a bounded evidence bundle with source identities, page or
    span locators, ranks, warnings, and truncation information.
16. If the intended source is absent or no evidence supports the request, the
    system records `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` or a source-acquisition gap.

### Citation and drafting proposal

17. The system aligns candidate evidence with the exact manuscript claim or
    writing need.
18. A citation proposal records:

    - manuscript revision and source span;
    - claim or writing-need identity;
    - candidate reference identity;
    - proposed citation key, if one exists;
    - whether that key is already present in the bibliography;
    - exact supporting pages or spans;
    - relevant equations, figures, or tables;
    - retrieval and proposal status;
    - warnings and unresolved conflicts; and
    - the reason the candidate may or may not support the claim.

19. A drafting proposal may suggest an outline, transition, explanation, or
    replacement passage, but every literature-dependent statement remains
    linked to evidence-bundle entries.
20. Original manuscript TeX is retained beside any rendered equation. Rendered
    HTML, MathML, or KaTeX is a review aid, not the manuscript authority.
21. Calculated manuscript results remain linked to their computational
    artifacts and are not misrepresented as literature claims.

### Human review

22. The local interface presents, together:

    - the exact manuscript passage;
    - rendered and original equation forms;
    - each candidate source page or span;
    - source metadata and citation-key status;
    - the proposed support relationship;
    - extraction and source warnings; and
    - prior decisions for the same manuscript identity.

23. The author or citation reviewer records a disposition such as accept for
    drafting, reject, defer, needs another source, or insufficient evidence.
24. The decision is appended as a new revision. It does not overwrite prior
    review history.
25. The system produces a review report or proposed patch artifact.
26. The manuscript remains unchanged unless a separate, explicit authority
    permits a bounded write operation.

## Corpus-role rules

| Corpus role | Retrieval use | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| `reference` | Final support and citation | Source-linked evidence |
| `curated_distillation` | Discovery and query expansion | Derived; not primary support |
| `query_material` | Manuscript claims and writing context | Never evidence for the same manuscript |
| `calculation` | Support for manuscript-derived results | Distinct from literature evidence |
| `generated_proposal` | Drafting and citation suggestions | Unreviewed derived output |
| `review_decision` | Human disposition history | Purpose- and revision-scoped |

The same bytes must not silently participate under multiple roles in one
retrieval operation.

## Claim and evidence distinctions

### Literature claim

A statement attributed to prior work requires reference evidence with an
inspectable locator.

### Calculated result

A value or conclusion produced by the manuscript's own computation requires a
link to the calculation protocol, inputs, outputs, and review state. A nearby
literature citation does not convert it into a literature result.

### Mixed claim

A statement combining a literature premise with a manuscript calculation must
retain both evidence paths separately.

### Editorial statement

Motivation, signposting, or exposition may not require a citation, but an LLM
cannot make that determination authoritatively.

## Citation-support behavior

### No self-citation through retrieval

The current manuscript is query material, not reference evidence. Retrieval
must not return manuscript passages as support for claims in that manuscript.

### No automatic citation insertion

A high retrieval score, valid citation key, or positive model classification
is insufficient to modify the manuscript or bibliography.

### No invented locators

Generated prose cites evidence-bundle entries. Citation validation confirms
that referenced evidence and locators exist.

### No silent source substitution

If the requested or intended source is absent, the system reports the gap. It
does not silently replace the source with a merely similar document.

### Source-diverse candidates

The candidate set should normally include the strongest relevant passage from
several sources before adding repeated passages from one source.

### Equation-aware review

When a claim depends on an equation, the review interface preserves:

- the manuscript's exact TeX;
- the source's admitted equation representation;
- definitions and assumptions near both equations;
- source-specific conventions; and
- any reconstruction or rendering warnings.

Visual similarity alone does not establish mathematical equivalence or
citation support.

## Alternative and failure scenarios

### A. No supporting reference is retrieved

The proposal is marked `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. The system may suggest a new
query or record a source-acquisition need, but it does not invent support.

### B. The strongest source is absent from the bibliography

The proposal reports the missing key or missing bibliographic entry. It does
not edit the bibliography.

### C. The source supports only part of the claim

The review identifies the supported and unsupported portions. It may recommend
splitting or narrowing the claim.

### D. Sources disagree

All relevant evidence and conventions remain visible. A drafting proposal
states the disagreement rather than merging the values silently.

### E. The passage is a manuscript-derived calculation

The system routes evidence review to the calculation artifacts and keeps
literature context separate.

### F. Equation extraction is uncertain

The source page, literal extraction, and reconstruction warning are shown. The
candidate cannot be accepted solely because the reconstructed equation
renders.

### G. The manuscript changes during review

Prior decisions remain bound to the earlier manuscript identity. The system
requires an explicit carry-forward or re-review rather than applying stale
decisions automatically.

### H. A write attempt lacks authority

The manuscript remains unchanged, and the denied attempt is recorded according
to the applicable runtime policy.

## Candidate artifact flow

```text
read-only manuscript revision
  -> exact manuscript parse
  -> claim and writing-need candidates
  -> reference-only retrieval requests

authorized reference evidence
  -> source-diverse evidence bundles
  -> claim-to-evidence alignment
  -> citation and drafting proposals

proposal plus manuscript context
  -> local human review
  -> append-only decision revision
  -> review report or proposed patch
  -> separately authorized manuscript action, if any
```

Workflow state contains compact artifact references. It does not contain source
bytes, manuscript text, private paths, full evidence bundles, or credentials.

## Required provenance

Every proposal must make it possible to recover:

- manuscript content and revision identity;
- exact manuscript source span;
- claim or writing-need identity;
- query and retrieval identities;
- corpus-role filters;
- evidence-bundle identity;
- candidate reference and bibliographic identities;
- exact source pages or spans;
- equation, figure, or table references where applicable;
- proposal engine and configuration identity;
- warnings and insufficiency findings; and
- append-only human decision revisions.

## Acceptance evidence for the pilot

A private reviewed benchmark must include:

- uncited literature claims;
- correctly cited claims;
- citations that support only part of a claim;
- equation-oriented claims;
- source-specific notation;
- manuscript-derived calculations;
- missing bibliography keys;
- intended sources absent from the corpus;
- conflicting sources;
- deliberately unsupported claims; and
- manuscript revisions that invalidate earlier review positions.

The pilot is technically demonstrated only when evidence shows:

1. the manuscript remains byte-for-byte unchanged during review;
2. manuscript passages never appear as reference support for themselves;
3. every proposed citation resolves to reference evidence and exact locators;
4. note-assisted discovery resolves final support back to source evidence;
5. calculated and literature claims remain distinct;
6. missing sources and bibliography entries are reported without mutation;
7. equation review retains original TeX and source warnings;
8. decisions are append-only and manuscript-revision-bound;
9. unsupported cases return `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`;
10. generated drafting proposals identify their evidence dependencies;
11. no private path, manuscript excerpt, source excerpt, or review payload enters
    public logs or fixtures; and
12. no manuscript, bibliography, citation, or publication action occurs without
    its own explicit authority.

Retrieval metrics and thresholds are versioned before evaluation. A successful
benchmark does not itself authorize manuscript changes or publication.

## Postconditions

On success:

- the author receives evidence-linked writing or citation proposals;
- every literature-dependent proposal can be traced to source evidence;
- unsupported and partially supported claims remain visible;
- calculation evidence remains separate from literature evidence;
- review history is preserved by manuscript revision;
- the manuscript and bibliography remain unchanged; and
- the author retains scientific and editorial control.

## Out of scope

UC-02 does not authorize or require:

- autonomous manuscript editing;
- automatic citation or bibliography insertion;
- automatic claim acceptance;
- automatic scientific-validity judgments;
- automatic acquisition of missing sources;
- publication, submission, release, or artifact publication;
- model training on private manuscript content;
- feedback of generated prose into the reference-evidence corpus;
- replacement of computational reproducibility with literature retrieval; or
- use of the manuscript as evidence for itself.
