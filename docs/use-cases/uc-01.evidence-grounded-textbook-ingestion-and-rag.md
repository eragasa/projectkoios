# UC-01: Evidence-grounded textbook ingestion and RAG

## Record status

- **Identifier:** `UC-01`
- **Definition status:** Draft
- **Observed basis:** a manually reviewed, equation-heavy semiconductor-physics
  textbook pilot
- **Architecture:**
  [evidence-grounded scientific retrieval](../adr.20260918.evidence-grounded-scientific-rag.md)
- **Parent roadmap:** `RAG-ROADMAP-01`

This record describes a product use case. It does not accept a contract,
authorize implementation or ingestion, approve a private source for use, or
establish scientific correctness.

## Goal

A researcher asks a technical question about an authorized textbook source and
receives a concise answer grounded in inspectable source evidence. The answer
preserves equations, source-specific notation, warnings, and exact page or span
provenance.

Curated notes may improve discovery and interpretation, but they do not replace
the source PDF or become primary evidence.

## Why this is the first use case

The pilot material exercises the difficult parts of scientific ingestion and
retrieval in one bounded corpus:

- native PDF text with imperfect reading order;
- chapter, section, and subsection hierarchy;
- inline and display mathematics;
- figures, captions, and tables;
- physical PDF pages that differ from printed page numbers;
- source-specific mathematical conventions;
- cross-section and cross-source relationships;
- numerical and terminological inconsistencies in the source; and
- reviewed explanatory notes with source-page links and figure embeds.

A successful implementation is therefore useful beyond textbooks while still
being small enough to inspect manually.

## Actors

### Primary actor

**Researcher** — asks a question, inspects evidence, and decides whether the
answer is useful.

### Supporting actors

- **Source reviewer** — reviews extraction, equation, warning, and citation
  evidence when required.
- **Ingestion system** — produces immutable, source-linked derived artifacts.
- **Search system** — indexes and ranks evidence without generating answers.
- **Generation client** — drafts an answer from a bounded evidence bundle.
- **Local review interface** — presents answers, pages, equations, figures,
  warnings, and provenance.
- **Deployment adapter** — performs authorized storage and execution effects.

## Preconditions

1. A machine-local configuration identifies an authorized private data root.
2. The source PDF has an immutable content identity and acquisition record.
3. Source access is authorized for the requested local operation.
4. Inputs and destinations are regular files or directories, not symlinks.
5. The source remains read-only during ingestion.
6. Every derived generation has an explicit identity and source dependency.
7. Corpus roles distinguish source evidence from curated notes and generated
   material.
8. Required ingestion and search contracts are identified with their actual
   lifecycle status; use does not imply acceptance.

## Trigger

The researcher submits a scientific information need, for example:

- locate and explain a definition or equation;
- compare related formulas from two sections;
- find the source convention governing a symbol;
- retrieve a figure or table relevant to a concept;
- identify the assumptions behind an approximation; or
- determine whether the source contains conflicting statements or values.

## Main success scenario

### Ingestion

1. The deployment adapter resolves the configured private data root and fails
   closed if no valid root is configured.
2. The source is registered by content identity. A private source locator is
   retained as provenance but is not used as identity.
3. The ingestion system verifies source bytes and emits an immutable literal
   transcript generation.
4. Literal extraction preserves page boundaries, block order evidence,
   bounding boxes, and extraction warnings.
5. The ingestion system emits a document-structure generation containing
   chapters, sections, equations, figures, captions, and tables.
6. Native mathematical text, rendered evidence, reconstructed symbolic forms,
   and interpretations remain separate representations.
7. Any reconstructed equation remains `AUTOMATED_UNREVIEWED` until separately
   reviewed.
8. Source-derived figures are represented by page and bounding-box provenance.
   Tight local crops may be emitted as derived aids; whole-page screenshots are
   not substituted for figure evidence.
9. Reviewed Markdown notes are parsed into a separate generation containing
   headings, exact Markdown or LaTeX, wiki links, PDF-page links, image embeds,
   and review warnings.
10. The ingestion system emits separate source-evidence and curated-note chunk
    generations.
11. Every chunk retains immutable links to its source generation and exact
    source page, block, span, equation, figure, or table evidence.

### Retrieval

12. The search system builds a deterministic lexical index over source-evidence
    units and records its index identity and ranking configuration.
13. Curated-note chunks are indexed under a distinct discovery-only corpus
    role.
14. Optional equation or semantic lanes participate only under a documented
    quality policy and retain their own model and index identities.
15. The researcher submits a query.
16. Search may use curated notes to identify terminology, sections, and source
    locators, but it resolves final candidates back to source-evidence units.
17. Search returns a bounded evidence bundle containing source identities,
    locators, ranks, warnings, and truncation information.
18. If evidence is absent or below the applicable gate, search reports
    `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` rather than manufacturing support.

### Generation and review

19. A generation client receives only the bounded evidence bundle required for
    the request.
20. The client drafts an answer whose citations reference evidence-bundle
    entries rather than invented filenames or page numbers.
21. Citation validation confirms that evidence identities and locators resolve
    and that any quotation occurs in retained evidence.
22. The local interface presents the answer beside the exact source page or
    span, original equation evidence, relevant figure, and warnings.
23. The researcher accepts, rejects, or revises the answer for the current
    purpose. This decision does not edit a manuscript, change source authority,
    or establish general scientific acceptance.

## Representation and authority rules

| Representation | Permitted role | Authority limit |
|---|---|---|
| Source PDF | Primary source | Authoritative source bytes; not automatically scientifically correct |
| Literal transcript | Searchable evidence | Derived extraction; may contain extraction defects |
| Structure map | Navigation and evidence organization | Derived; boundaries may require review |
| Equation candidate | Equation discovery | Never accepted merely because it renders |
| Figure crop | Local visual aid | Derived from a page and bounding box |
| Curated note | Discovery and explanation | Must resolve claims back to source evidence |
| Search result | Ranked evidence candidate | Does not assert relevance or truth |
| Generated answer | Reviewable synthesis | Never primary evidence |
| Human decision | Purpose-scoped disposition | Does not silently alter other authorities |

The system must never flatten these representations into one undifferentiated
text corpus.

### Worked-example and problem-material boundary

Numbered examples embedded in textbook prose are source structure, not the
same content class as end-of-chapter exercises, discussion questions, or
solutions. An example inventory therefore records, without copying source
prose:

- a stable source-scoped example identity;
- chapter and example number;
- structural kind, such as standard or conceptual example;
- physical source-page span;
- detector and inventory-generation identity;
- inclusion or exclusion disposition; and
- completeness and review status.

A generated example note is a derived proposal. It remains
`AUTOMATED_UNREVIEWED` until separately reviewed even when its source locator
resolves, its equations render, and its numerical result agrees with the
source. Model generation must not serve as authoritative extraction or promote
a note into the source-evidence role.

Worked-example, exercise, question, and solution notes are excluded from
ordinary retrieval by default. A separately authorized product mode may admit
them under a distinct corpus role, but source-grounded answers must still
resolve support to the source PDF. This policy prevents generated solutions
from becoming circular evidence for their own claims.

Batch publication requires a frozen inventory, collision detection before the
first write, explicit replacement authority for every existing target,
same-filesystem atomic writes, and post-publication content verification.
Validation and publication success do not imply mathematical, pedagogical, or
scientific acceptance.

## Candidate artifact flow

```text
source bytes
  -> source manifest
  -> literal transcript generation
  -> document-structure generation
  -> source-evidence chunk generation
  -> deterministic source index

reviewed notes and figure aids
  -> parsed-note generation
  -> curated-note chunk generation
  -> discovery index

optional worked-example inventory
  -> source-linked generated-note candidates
  -> validation and collision dispositions
  -> private publication receipts
  -> excluded-by-default problem-material corpus role

query
  -> source and discovery retrieval lanes
  -> deterministic fusion
  -> bounded evidence bundle
  -> generated draft
  -> citation validation
  -> human review
```

These names describe use-case artifacts. Exact schemas and operation identities
remain contracts owned by their component repositories.

## Required provenance

Every final evidence unit must make it possible to recover:

- corpus and source identity;
- source-byte digest;
- extraction and derivation generation identities;
- physical PDF page index;
- printed page label when available;
- source block, span, or bounding box;
- chapter and section path;
- content kind such as prose, equation, figure, caption, table, worked example,
  exercise, question, or solution;
- source-structure subtype and inventory-generation identity when applicable;
- exact indexed representation;
- corpus role, exclusion disposition, and review status;
- generation, validation, collision, and publication receipts for generated
  note candidates when applicable;
- transformation, exclusion, and warning references; and
- retrieval lane ranks and final ordering.

Private paths and locators remain in private storage and are not placed in
public issue bodies, logs, fixtures, or generated documentation.

## Retrieval behavior

### Source-first citation

A note hit may route retrieval to a relevant section, but a generated answer
must cite a source-evidence unit. A note-only result is insufficient for a
source-grounded answer.

### Equation-aware retrieval

An equation retrieval unit should keep together:

- the equation representation admitted by policy;
- symbol definitions;
- immediately relevant assumptions;
- nearby explanatory text; and
- exact source locators.

Chunking must not join unrelated equations or prose across unsupported source
boundaries.

### Convention preservation

Source-specific conventions remain scoped to their source and section. Search
must not silently normalize incompatible conventions across documents.

### Disagreement preservation

When two source locations disagree, retrieval returns both evidence records and
the warning. Ranking or generation must not silently choose a value and erase
the conflict.

### Deterministic baseline

Lexical retrieval is evaluated before optional dense retrieval. Fusion, tie
breaking, lane participation, and index identity must be replayable.

## Alternative and failure scenarios

### A. Literal extraction is malformed

The system retains the literal block, records the warning, and may emit a
separate reconstruction candidate. It does not rewrite the source evidence.

### B. An equation renders but is not verified

The rendered form remains a derived aid. Rendering success does not promote the
candidate or establish mathematical equivalence.

### C. Retrieval finds only a curated note

The system follows the note's provenance to source evidence. If that resolution
fails, the answer is returned as insufficiently evidenced.

### D. Source locations disagree

The evidence bundle includes the conflicting locations and warning. The answer
states the disagreement without silently reconciling it.

### E. A figure cannot be associated reliably

The figure remains unassociated or candidate-status. The system does not infer
a caption or section link merely from proximity.

### F. An effect has ambiguous completion

The workflow records the ambiguity and enters query-only reconciliation. It
must not retry merely because a lease expired or a worker disappeared.

### G. The configured data root is absent or invalid

All writes fail closed. Read behavior follows the separately defined
configuration and authorization policy.

### H. Evidence is inadequate

The system returns `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. Retrieval failure is not evidence
that a claim is false or irrelevant.

### I. A new heading class changes the example inventory

The system emits a new inventory generation and a reviewed difference. It does
not silently preserve an earlier completeness claim or reinterpret existing
identities in place.

### J. A generated note fails structural, mathematical, or provenance checks

The candidate is rejected, retained only according to private run-evidence
policy, and excluded from publication and retrieval. A successful model call
is not a successful note generation.

### K. A publication target already exists

The candidate is staged for review and receives a collision disposition. The
system does not replace the existing note without exact, separately recorded
replacement authority.

## Acceptance evidence for the pilot

A reviewed private benchmark must cover:

- exact-source and paraphrased queries;
- section and page retrieval;
- equation-oriented questions;
- figure and table retrieval;
- source-specific notation;
- cross-section synthesis;
- known source discrepancies;
- deliberately insufficient evidence;
- source-evidence versus curated-note filtering; and
- default exclusion of worked-example, exercise, question, and solution notes.

The pilot is technically demonstrated only when evidence shows:

1. source bytes remain unchanged;
2. immutable generation identities replay deterministically;
3. every evaluated source citation resolves to the expected page or span;
4. note-assisted retrieval resolves final support back to source evidence;
5. equations retain their representation and review status;
6. figure references resolve to source pages and bounding boxes;
7. known warnings and disagreements remain retrievable;
8. ranking and fusion replay with identical ordering;
9. insufficient-evidence cases do not produce unsupported answers;
10. worked-example and problem-material records cannot enter ordinary
    retrieval without a separately authorized corpus role;
11. any generated note admitted to private publication retains its generation,
    validation, collision, and review dispositions;
12. no private path, source excerpt, or protected payload enters public logs or
    fixtures; and
13. benchmark thresholds are versioned before evaluation rather than selected
    after observing the result.

Real documents supplement synthetic regression fixtures. They do not replace
small public fixtures that isolate parser, identity, ranking, and failure
behavior.

## Postconditions

On success:

- the source remains immutable;
- every derived artifact is attributable to a source and generation;
- the researcher receives an inspectable evidence-grounded answer;
- answer citations resolve to source evidence;
- notes remain distinguishable from evidence;
- warnings and disagreements remain visible; and
- review decisions are recorded without editing the source or a manuscript.

## Out of scope

UC-01 does not authorize or require:

- bulk ingestion of every available private PDF;
- automatic source correction;
- automatic equation acceptance;
- automatic scientific or citation acceptance;
- automatic mathematical or pedagogical acceptance of generated solutions;
- bulk generation or publication of textbook worked-example notes;
- manuscript editing;
- bibliography modification;
- publication or release;
- cloud upload of private artifacts;
- a vector-only retrieval architecture;
- a specific LLM;
- CPN authority; or
- migration of an existing private corpus.
